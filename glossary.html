Production & Broadcast Glossary

Plain-English definitions for the deliverables, project types, and technical terms that come up when we're figuring out what a project actually needs.

Deliverables

What you actually walk away with, depending on the project.

Finished edit
The final, polished video, ready to publish — color corrected, sound mixed, graphics and titles in, exported in whatever format you need. This is "the product," as opposed to raw or rough footage.

Live stream
The live broadcast itself, delivered in real time to a platform (YouTube, Zoom, Facebook, a private embedded player) as the event happens — not a file delivered afterward.

Social cutdowns
Short, re-edited versions of a longer video, trimmed and reformatted for social feeds. Same source footage, shorter runtime, different aspect ratio and pacing — e.g. a 3-minute brand film becomes a 15–30 second vertical clip for Instagram, LinkedIn, or TikTok.

Highlight reel
A short, high-energy recap of an event or project — the best moments cut together, usually a few minutes long. Used to promote an event afterward or summarize it for people who weren't there. (Sometimes called a "sizzle reel.")

Podcast episodes
Audio, or audio-plus-video, episodes edited and packaged for podcast distribution — trimmed, leveled, often with intro/outro music, ready to publish to podcast platforms.

Audio-only mix
A standalone audio version of the production, mixed and mastered on its own without picture. Useful for radio, a podcast feed, or accessibility.

Raw footage
The original, unedited camera and audio files exactly as captured — no cutting, no color work, no mixing. For a client who wants to do their own editing, or wants an archive of everything shot.

Captions
The spoken dialogue as text, synced to the video — either burned into the picture (open captions) or as a selectable track (closed captions). Matters for accessibility and for social platforms, where sound is often off by default.

Video Production

Brand or promotional film
A polished video that markets an organization, product, or service — meant to build awareness or persuade. Not a record of an event; a piece of marketing.

Training or internal comms
Video made for people inside an organization — onboarding, how-to or process training, a leadership message. Not intended for public marketing.

Testimonial or interview series
A set of on-camera interviews — clients, employees, or outside experts — used to build credibility. Client testimonials, case-study style content, "meet the team" pieces.

Podcast or recurring series
An ongoing, episodic production: the same format, recorded and released repeatedly, rather than a one-off video.

ProAV Production

Conference or annual meeting
Full live-event production for a conference, member meeting, or shareholder event — staging, screens, audio, multi-camera coverage of a scheduled program with an agenda of speakers.

Press event or announcement
Live coverage of a press conference or public announcement. Typically shorter and more time-sensitive than a conference, often produced for immediate media pickup.

Concert or performance
Multi-camera live production of a musical or stage performance — camera switching, stage lighting and screens. Usually the highest production value in the ProAV category.

Event coverage
General-purpose live documentation of an event — a gala, an awards show, a conference — without necessarily requiring an elaborate multi-camera control room.

Cloud & REMI Production

Zoom Event
A production run through Zoom's own Events platform — I manage the production behind a standard Zoom Event or Webinar (speakers, screen shares, Q&A) for the audience watching inside Zoom.

Zoom ISO Switched Broadcast
A more advanced version of the above: every individual source — each speaker's camera, each screen share — is recorded as its own isolated ("ISO") track, in addition to the live-switched program feed. After the event, I can go back and re-cut a polished program from the raw isolated feeds, rather than being limited to only what was switched live.

Venue/MC SRT transport to Cloud
The workflow behind the transport diagram on my Cloud & REMI panel: a signal from the venue or the MC's camera is sent over SRT (Secure Reliable Transport — a low-latency internet video protocol) up to cloud infrastructure, then routed into the production switcher as if it were a local camera. This makes it possible to produce a show using a contribution feed from a remote physical location without a satellite truck or a dedicated fiber line.

Other

Something else
If your project doesn't fit neatly into the categories above, or it's a hybrid of a few of them, that's completely normal — just describe it in your own words when you reach out.
